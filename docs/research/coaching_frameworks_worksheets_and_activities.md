# Coaching Frameworks for Educational Leadership
## Companion Worksheets and End-of-Chapter Activities

A practical workbook to accompany the research synthesis. Each chapter
pairs a reflection worksheet with a fun, low-prep activity you can run
solo, in a coaching dyad, or with a full cohort.

How to use: Read the related framework section in the research report,
complete the worksheet, then run the activity. Most activities take
15–40 minutes.

---

## Chapter 1 — Blended Coaching (Bloom & Wilson)

### Worksheet 1.1: The Stance Diagnostic

For each current problem of practice on your desk, decide which stance
is called for. Circle one and justify it in a sentence.

| # | Problem of Practice (one line) | Stance | Why this stance? |
|---|--------------------------------|--------|------------------|
| 1 |                                | Facilitative / Collaborative / Consultative / Instructional | |
| 2 |                                | Facilitative / Collaborative / Consultative / Instructional | |
| 3 |                                | Facilitative / Collaborative / Consultative / Instructional | |

**Check yourself:** If you circled "Facilitative" for every row, ask —
does the leader actually have the base knowledge to discover a good
solution? If not, a directive move is the honest choice.

### Worksheet 1.2: The Calibration Log

After your next three coaching conversations, log what stance you used,
what you saw in the leader, and what you would change.

- Conversation 1 — Stance used: ___  Signal it worked/didn't: ___  Adjustment: ___
- Conversation 2 — Stance used: ___  Signal it worked/didn't: ___  Adjustment: ___
- Conversation 3 — Stance used: ___  Signal it worked/didn't: ___  Adjustment: ___

### End-of-Chapter Activity: **Stance Switch**

*Group of 3. 20 minutes.*

1. One person plays the leader and brings a real 90-second dilemma.
2. One person plays the coach.
3. The third is the "Stance Caller." Every 60 seconds the Stance Caller
   says a new stance out loud — "Facilitative!" "Instructional!" — and
   the coach must switch mid-conversation without breaking rapport.
4. After 4 minutes, rotate roles.

Debrief: Which stance was hardest to hold? Which felt most natural?
When did the switch *help* the leader, and when did it jar them?

## Chapter 2 — Cognitive Coaching (Costa & Garmston)

### Worksheet 2.1: The Advice Urge Tracker

For one week, tally every time you feel the urge to give advice in a
coaching conversation.

| Trigger (what did they say?) | Advice I wanted to give | What I said instead |
|------------------------------|-------------------------|---------------------|
|                              |                         |                     |
|                              |                         |                     |
|                              |                         |                     |

At the end of the week, look for patterns: which topics pull your
advice reflex hardest? Those are your growth edges.

### Worksheet 2.2: The Three-Verb Drill

Cognitive Coaching runs on three verbal tools: **pause, paraphrase,
probe**. Rewrite the following coach turns to use only those three
tools.

Leader: "I don't know what to do about this teacher. Nothing I try works."

- **Pause** (5+ seconds of silence — what do you expect to happen?): ___
- **Paraphrase** (reflect the essence, not the words): ___
- **Probe** (open-ended, presupposing capability): ___

### End-of-Chapter Activity: **The Silent Coach**

*Pairs. 15 minutes.*

1. The leader talks for 3 minutes about a real problem.
2. The coach may only **pause, nod, or paraphrase in fewer than 10
   words**. No questions. No advice.
3. Swap.

Debrief: What emerged for the leader when the coach refused to rescue?
Most participants report that solutions they "couldn't find" surfaced
within 90 seconds of being heard.

## Chapter 3 — NISL Executive Development Program

### Worksheet 3.1: Executive Problem Framing

Pick one school-level problem you are currently carrying. Frame it at
three altitudes.

- **Symptom altitude** (what you see day to day): ___
- **System altitude** (what structure, routine, or resource allocation produces this symptom?): ___
- **Strategy altitude** (what 18-month move would make this class of symptom rare?): ___

A common NISL insight: principals over-invest at the symptom altitude
and under-invest at the strategy altitude.

### Worksheet 3.2: The Transfer Plan

After every NISL-style module or case study, fill this in within 24
hours.

- One concept I will apply this week: ___
- The specific situation I will apply it in: ___
- What my coach will look for when they observe me: ___
- Evidence that it worked: ___

### End-of-Chapter Activity: **Case Study Hot Seat**

*Cohort of 4–8. 30 minutes.*

1. One principal presents a live dilemma in exactly 2 minutes
   (set a timer).
2. The cohort asks only **clarifying questions** for 3 minutes.
3. The cohort silently writes one executive-level recommendation each.
4. Each recommendation is read aloud without debate.
5. The presenter picks the recommendation that surprises them most and
   explains why.

No one is allowed to defend their recommendation. The point is to let
the presenter's own judgment do the picking.

## Chapter 4 — ISAL Superintendent Cohort Model

### Worksheet 4.1: The Isolation Audit

Superintendent isolation is structural, not personal. Map yours.

- Peers I talk to monthly about real dilemmas (not just logistics): ___
- Topics I currently have **no one** to think out loud with: ___
- Last time I was genuinely surprised by another leader's perspective: ___

If the third line is over 60 days old, your cohort has gone dormant —
re-engage deliberately.

### Worksheet 4.2: The Peer Coaching Contract

Before a peer coaching partnership begins, both parties sign off on:

- Frequency: ___
- Duration of each call: ___
- Confidentiality clause (exactly what stays in the room): ___
- Off-limits topics (if any): ___
- How we'll end this partnership if it stops serving us: ___

### End-of-Chapter Activity: **Governance Dilemma Roulette**

*Cohort of 4–10. 40 minutes.*

1. Each participant writes one anonymized governance dilemma on an
   index card (board dynamics, finance, community, politics).
2. Shuffle. Deal one card to each person — not their own.
3. Each person takes 3 minutes to present the dilemma **as if it were
   their own**, in first person.
4. The cohort offers peer-coaching questions — not answers.
5. Original authors reveal themselves last and say what they heard that
   they couldn't have thought of alone.

The point: dilemmas feel different when you hold them in someone else's
body. The exercise builds both empathy and range.

## Chapter 5 — EI-Centric and Situational Coaching

### Worksheet 5.1: The Regulation Map

Track your week.

| Day | Highest-stress moment | Body signal | What I did | What worked |
|-----|----------------------|-------------|-----------|-------------|
| Mon |                      |             |           |             |
| Tue |                      |             |           |             |
| Wed |                      |             |           |             |
| Thu |                      |             |           |             |
| Fri |                      |             |           |             |

Look for the body signal that *precedes* dysregulation — jaw, shoulders,
breath. That's your early-warning system.

### Worksheet 5.2: The Four-Quadrant EI Check

Rate yourself 1–5 this week and write one evidence example.

- **Self-awareness** (1–5): ___  Example: ___
- **Self-management** (1–5): ___  Example: ___
- **Social awareness** (1–5): ___  Example: ___
- **Relationship management** (1–5): ___  Example: ___

Your lowest quadrant is this week's coaching focus, not your highest.

### End-of-Chapter Activity: **The Dysregulation Drill**

*Pairs. 20 minutes.*

1. Partner A plays a dysregulated staff member (angry parent, union rep,
   frustrated teacher). They pick an intensity: 3, 5, or 8 out of 10.
2. Partner B is the leader. Their only goal: stay regulated for 3
   minutes. They do not have to solve the problem, agree, or concede.
3. Use a stopwatch. If B loses regulation (voice tightens, rushes,
   interrupts, placates), A says "reset" and they restart.
4. Swap.

Debrief: What intensity broke you? What helped you recover? Regulation
is a trainable muscle, not a trait.

## Chapter 6 — NYC Leadership Academy Facilitative Competency-Based Coaching

### Worksheet 6.1: The Low-Inference Scripting Drill

Low-inference means writing what a camera would see and a microphone
would record — no adjectives, no interpretations.

Watch a 5-minute segment of any meeting or classroom. Script it in two
columns.

| Time | What was said or done (low-inference) | Interpretation I want to make (separate!) |
|------|----------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------|
| 0:00 |                                        |                                           |
| 1:00 |                                        |                                           |

If an adjective sneaks into the left column, move it right. The left
column is evidence; the right column is inference.

### Worksheet 6.2: The Competency Crosswalk

Pick one competency from your district's leadership framework (or LPPW).

- Competency: ___
- Three low-inference behaviors that would count as evidence of this competency: 1) ___  2) ___  3) ___
- Three low-inference behaviors that would *not* count (common false positives): 1) ___  2) ___  3) ___

### End-of-Chapter Activity: **Scripting Showdown**

*Groups of 3. 25 minutes.*

1. Play a 4-minute video clip of any meeting, classroom, or TV
   workplace scene.
2. All three participants script independently.
3. Compare scripts line by line. Any adjective, motive, or
   interpretation that appears — strike it through and replace with
   camera-microphone language.
4. Score: one point per successfully stripped inference; one point
   per interpretation you caught in a partner's script that they missed.

The team with the tightest, least-inferential script wins. Prize: they
coach first next week.

## Cross-Framework Capstone Activity: **The Hybrid Coach**

*Cohort of 4–8. 45 minutes.*

1. One participant brings a single real dilemma.
2. Assign each of the remaining participants one framework as their
   "coaching lens."
3. The leader describes the dilemma for 3 minutes.
4. Each framework coach gets **90 seconds** to respond from their lens
   — a Cognitive Coach only paraphrases and probes, a Blended Coach
   names a stance, an NISL coach reframes to system or strategy
   altitude, an ISAL coach surfaces a peer parallel, an EI coach names
   the emotional pattern, an NYCLA coach offers a low-inference
   observation.
5. The leader picks the two responses that shifted their thinking
   most and reflects on why.

Debrief: Which lens did the problem *need*? Which lens did you default
to, and what does that reveal about your own coaching identity?

## Facilitator Notes

- **Timebox ruthlessly.** Under-running is better than over-running.
  A tight 3-minute drill energizes; a loose 20-minute one deflates.
- **Protect psychological safety.** Confidentiality is the precondition
  for honest practice; name it at the start of every session.
- **Practice before polish.** Expect the first run of each drill to
  feel awkward. That awkwardness is the learning.
- **Rotate roles.** Everyone plays leader, coach, and observer in a
  full cycle; role-taking builds perspective faster than discussion.
- **Debrief is where the learning crystallizes.** Budget at least
  one-third of every activity for debrief, not just practice.
